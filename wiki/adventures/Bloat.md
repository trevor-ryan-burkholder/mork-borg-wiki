# Bloat — A Gluttonous Dungeon
**Type:** adventure / scenario
**Source:** MBC Bloat

Silas, a vagabond, sought shelter from a storm in a large animal burrow. To his surprise he found a set of stairs leading to an underground labyrinth, once the home of an obscure cult of bacchanalian priests dedicated to a [[Goddess of Fat and Plenty]]. In the chapel he found a beautiful knife and fork — the [[Gourmand's Cutlery]] — and a statue that compelled him to use them.

The Cutlery allows the wielder to eat **anything**. Which is good, as the user becomes famished and must eat. Luckily, everything tastes wonderful. Silas was overcome. All he wants to do is eat. He compelled two [[Fleshy Automaton]]s of the labyrinth to serve him; now they are creating more of their kind, kidnapping people to feed to their Fattened King.

The hook can be the disappeared villagers' relatives or lords; the PCs stumbling on the burrow while hiding from brigands or inquisitors; or someone hunting [[Silas the Fattened King]] specifically.

Six rooms: the Vestibule (cramped, ratbadger-dug crawlspace where armor won't fit); the Rotting Larder (where Fleshy Automatons prepare gourmet feasts of decayed meat); the Sewage Pit (where Silas's filth has become a sentient sludge — a Fecal Acid creature that cannot be killed, only dispersed with water); the Chapel of Filth (statue of a corpulent woman with opal eyes — look into them and you must visit Silas as priest); Automaton Storage (where bodies are made into automatons in huge vats of rendered fat); and The Fattened King — Silas himself, too large to fit through the door, fed by his automatons, wielding the Cutlery, with the moves **Engulf** and **Flatulence**.

Hidden under the Sewage Pit's sludge: d6 silver and a curved tongue-shaped knife (d6 damage, on hit makes the target ravenously hungry and desperately thirsty — must eat and drink within the hour or die).

**Connections:** [[Silas the Fattened King]], [[Gourmand's Cutlery]], [[Fleshy Automaton]], [[Goddess of Fat and Plenty]]

**Gaps/Expansion Notes:** The Goddess of Fat and Plenty is named only by domain — no proper name. The bacchanalian priests' history, their disappearance, and the labyrinth's geographic placement are all open.
