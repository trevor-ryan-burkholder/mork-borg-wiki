The Forgotten Mind-Cult

cosmology / lost faith MÖRK BORG CULT FERETORY: The Tablets of Ochre Obscurity, p.64

Older than the Two-Headed Basilisks. Older, perhaps, than Verhu's first prophecy. They worked clay from the Valley of the Unfortunate Undead — the same black soil that drags wanderers into the Death Realm — and pressed coded glyphs into it until it dried ochre. The Tablets of Ochre Obscurity are what remained when their faith ended.

What they were called, what they worshipped, what they did to deserve the silence — none of it survives. Only the relics. Only the codes.

What the Tablets Say About Them

The cult thought in registers no surviving language captures cleanly. The tablets reveal:

  • They had a theory of dreaming — that reality might not be — and could weaponize that doubt against a target.
  • They knew matter as something comprehensible, mendable, breakable — armor improved, weapons reshaped, by reading the right tablet aloud.
  • They could ping the shared subconscious of living things at 70 feet.
  • They practiced pure logic as divination — reading traps and Powers as if both were grammar.
  • They believed irrelevance was a force you could induce in another being. Make them mean nothing for d4 rounds and even their enemies will forget to attack.
  • They cracked the moon into water and called it Freezing Moon.
  • They wrote a tablet whose code is itself a mind-plague — choreomania, dance-madness, d4 rounds of involuntary movement.

The glyphs require Presence +3 to comprehend. Only the Forlorn Philosopher sees through them natively. This is the lone canonical thread tying any living tradition back to the mind-cult.

Where They Came From

The Valley clay says everything the source does not. The Valley is where HE coils. Where SHE dragged the dead. Where the Realm of the Dead reaches up through the soil to grab the living. The mind-cult worked that clay — pressed their relics from the same dirt that grows undeath like a crop.

They may have known the basilisks. They may have known the Dead Gods before SHE killed them. They may have been part of the slaughter. Source does not say.

Connections

Tablets of Ochre Obscurity, Valley of the Unfortunate Undead, Forlorn Philosopher, Two-Headed Basilisks, Dead Gods, SHE, HE, Death Realm, The Loathing of Vulark

Gaps / Expansion Notes

The cult is named only as forgotten. Their pantheon, their temples, their successors — all blank. The connection between the Forlorn Philosopher tradition and the mind-cult is mechanical only (Philosophers wield the tablets without the Presence requirement) — whether the Philosophers know they inherit from this cult, or merely happen to think in compatible patterns, is open. Vulark is named only in The Loathing of Vulark (tablet 9) — possibly a leader, possibly a victim. The clay-of-the-Valley relics may not be limited to the ten surviving tablets; canon flags "so rare" but not "the only ones."
